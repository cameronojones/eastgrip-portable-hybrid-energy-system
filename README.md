# EastGRIP Portable Hybrid Renewable Energy System

A team engineering project developing a modular wind-solar power system for the East Greenland Ice-core Project (EastGRIP), a remote Arctic research camp in north-east Greenland.

[View the complete design report](./EastGRIP_Final_Report.pdf)

## Project Overview

EastGRIP currently relies primarily on diesel generation. This project investigated whether a portable hybrid renewable-energy system could reduce fuel consumption while collecting environmental and performance data for future deployments.

The proposed system combines:

- Six bifacial photovoltaic panels
- A small horizontal-axis wind turbine
- Battery storage and power-conditioning equipment
- Environmental and electrical sensors
- Automated turbine protection and enclosure heating
- Modular structural supports suitable for transport by LC-130 aircraft

The design was developed for temperatures down to -30°C and wind speeds up to 40 knots.

## Predicted Performance

- Seasonal energy generation: approximately 2,850 kWh
- Solar contribution: approximately 91%
- Wind contribution: approximately 9%
- Estimated capital cost: £9,209
- Estimated diesel displacement: 670-1,072 litres per season
- Base-case payback period: approximately 1.6 operating seasons

These values are design estimates rather than results from a manufactured or field-tested prototype.

## Key Engineering Constraints

- Maximum total mass below 3 tonnes
- Maximum operating height below 4 metres
- Compatibility with aircraft-pallet transport
- Safe operation at temperatures down to -30°C
- Wind resistance and turbine protection up to 40 knots
- Internal battery and electronics temperature maintained at or above 0°C
- Modular seasonal assembly in a remote environment
- Low maintenance requirements and accessible sensor data
